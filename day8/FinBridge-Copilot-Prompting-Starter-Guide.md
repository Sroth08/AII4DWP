## The Good Prompt Formula

Every good Copilot prompt answers up to four simple questions. You do not need all four every time, but the more you answer, the better the result.

| Question to ask yourself | Example answer |
|--------------------------|----------------|
| **What do I want it to do?** | Summarise / draft / explain / shorten |
| **What is it working from?** | This email thread / the Q3 report / my notes below |
| **Who is this for?** | My manager / the whole team / a client |
| **How do I want it laid out?** | Bullet points / one paragraph / a short table |

Put together, that becomes: **"Summarise the key figures from the Q3 budget report as a bullet-point list. This is for my manager, so keep it brief and jargon-free."**

You do not need to follow a formula. Just picture the person you are writing for and tell Copilot what you need as if you were asking a helpful colleague.

## Copilot in Outlook

Copilot in Outlook can help you draft replies, summarise long email threads, and prepare for meetings. Here are three prompts you can use today.

---

**Example 1 — Summarise a long thread before jumping in**

> "Summarise this email thread. List the key decisions made, any open actions, and who is responsible for each."

Use this when you have been added to a long chain and need to catch up quickly without reading every message.

---

**Example 2 — Draft a reply when you are short on time**

> "Draft a professional reply to this email. Thank the sender, confirm I can attend the meeting on Thursday, and ask them to send the agenda in advance. Keep it brief and friendly."

Copilot will write the reply. Read it, adjust anything that does not sound like you, then send.

---

**Example 3 — Prepare for a meeting from your inbox**

> "I have a budget review meeting this afternoon. Look through my recent emails and summarise anything relevant I should be aware of before the meeting — key numbers, outstanding questions, or anything flagged as urgent."

Copilot will scan your inbox and pull together a quick briefing so you walk into the meeting prepared.

## Copilot in Word

Copilot in Word can help you draft documents, tidy up existing text, and change the tone or length of what you have written.

---

**Example 1 — Turn rough notes into a proper document**

> "Turn the notes below into a short summary document with a brief introduction, a few numbered key points, and a one-sentence conclusion. Use plain, professional language."

How to do this: type the prompt above into the Copilot panel, then on a new line paste in your bullet points or rough notes — just copy and paste them straight in after the prompt text. Copilot will read everything you have typed and turn it into a clean document you can then edit and share.

---

**Example 2 — Shorten a document without losing the key points**

> "This document is too long for a senior audience. Shorten it to no more than one page. Keep the three most important recommendations and remove anything that is background detail."

Useful when you have a detailed report but need a one-pager for an exec or a client.

---

**Example 3 — Change the tone of a draft**

> "Rewrite the section below in a warmer, more conversational tone. It currently sounds too formal for an internal team update. Keep all the same facts."

Great for situations where you have drafted something accurate but it reads stiffly — team announcements, internal newsletters, manager updates.

## Copilot in Excel

Copilot in Excel can help you understand data, highlight patterns, and explain what formulas do — without needing to be an Excel expert.

---

**Example 1 — Get a plain-English summary of a spreadsheet**

> "Summarise what this spreadsheet shows. What are the highest and lowest values in the Total column, and is there any obvious trend across the months?"

Use this when someone has sent you a spreadsheet and you need to understand it quickly without digging through every cell.

---

**Example 2 — Highlight unusual figures**

> "Look at the Expenses column. Are there any entries that seem unusually high compared to the others? List them with the amounts so I can take a closer look."

Useful for a quick sense-check on a budget or expense report before a meeting or sign-off. You do not need to know anything about averages or formulas — just describe what you are looking for in plain English.

---

**Example 3 — Explain a formula in plain English**

> "Explain what the formula in cell D14 does, in plain English. Assume I am not an Excel expert."

No more guessing what a formula does or why it is broken. Copilot will explain it clearly, and you can ask follow-up questions.

## Quick Tips for Better Results

**Be specific about length.** "Write a short summary" is vague. "Write a three-sentence summary" is clear.

**Tell it the audience.** "Write this for a non-finance senior manager" gives Copilot useful context it will use.

**If the first result is not right, ask again.** Try: *"Make it shorter"*, *"Use a more formal tone"*, or *"Focus on the risks, not the background."* You do not need to start over.

**Use it to start, not to finish.** Copilot gives you a first draft — think of it as a helpful starting point rather than a final answer. A quick read-through before you send or share anything is all it takes.

## A Note on Data Safety

Good news first: Microsoft 365 Copilot is designed to be safe to use for your everyday work. It works entirely within FinBridge's own Microsoft environment — your data stays inside our systems, the same way it does when you use Outlook or Word normally. You do not need to worry every time you use it.

There is one habit worth building, and it comes from the AI guidance you covered on Day 1 of this programme:

**Use Copilot (inside Microsoft 365) for work tasks. Do not use external AI tools — such as ChatGPT or Google Gemini — for anything work-related.**

The difference matters because external tools are outside FinBridge's control. If you paste a client's figures, a colleague's pay details, or anything from a confidential document into an external tool, that information leaves our systems. Copilot inside Microsoft 365 is the right place for that kind of work — it is built for it.

A simple way to remember it: if the information would feel sensitive to share in an email to someone outside the company, use Copilot rather than an external tool — and if you are unsure, just ask IT.

If you ever open a document and see a coloured banner near the top saying something like "Confidential" or "Highly Confidential", that is just a label telling Copilot and other systems how to handle it. You do not need to do anything differently — just be mindful that the content is sensitive and treat it the same way you would any other confidential document.
