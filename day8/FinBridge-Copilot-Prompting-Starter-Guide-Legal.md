Welcome to Microsoft 365 Copilot. This guide gives you real, ready-to-use prompts for your day-to-day legal work — no technical knowledge needed. The examples are written for the kinds of tasks you actually do: reviewing documents, managing correspondence, and making sense of dense files quickly.

There is nothing to install and nothing to learn before you start. Just open Outlook, Word, or Excel, find the Copilot button, and type what you need as if you were asking a capable colleague.

---

## How to Ask Copilot Well

You do not need a special formula. Just answer four plain questions before you type:

| Ask yourself | Example answer |
|--------------|----------------|
| **What do I want it to do?** | Summarise / draft / explain / compare |
| **What is it working from?** | This email thread / the NDA in this document / my notes below |
| **Who is this for?** | The partner / the client / an internal team update |
| **How do I want it laid out?** | Short paragraph / bullet points / a table |

A prompt built from those answers might look like this:

> *"Summarise the key obligations in this NDA. Present them as a bullet-point list. This is for a partner who needs a quick overview before a call."*

That is all there is to it. The more specific you are, the more useful the result. And if the first answer is not quite right, just ask again — *"make it shorter"* or *"focus on the termination clauses"* — you do not need to start over.

## Copilot in Outlook

Copilot in Outlook can help you get on top of long email threads, draft replies, and prepare for calls and meetings without spending an hour reading back through your inbox.

---

**Example 1 — Get up to speed on a long matter thread**

> "Summarise this email thread. What are the key issues discussed, any decisions made, and what is still outstanding or unresolved?"

Use this when you have been added to a long chain and need to understand the history before you reply or join a call.

---

**Example 2 — Draft a professional reply under time pressure**

> "Draft a reply to this email. Acknowledge the client's concerns, confirm we are reviewing the contract and will respond by Friday, and keep the tone professional but reassuring. Keep it to three short paragraphs."

Copilot will write the reply. Read it, adjust anything that does not sound like you or does not reflect your matter, then send.

---

**Example 3 — Prepare a quick briefing before a client call**

> "I have a call in 30 minutes about the [client name] contract dispute. Look through my recent emails mentioning [client name] and give me a short summary of where things stand, any key dates, and anything I should be ready to discuss."

Replace [client name] with the actual name you would search for in your inbox — a company name, a person's name, or a project name all work. Copilot will pull together a quick brief so you go into the call prepared rather than scrambling.

## Copilot in Word

Copilot in Word can help you draft, tighten, and restructure documents — useful whether you are starting from scratch, tidying up a first draft, or turning meeting notes into a proper document.

---

**Example 1 — Turn rough notes into a structured document**

> "Turn the notes below into a clear, professional summary. Include a short introduction, the main points as numbered paragraphs, and a brief conclusion. Use plain language — this is for a client, not internal use."

How to use this: type the prompt, then paste your bullet points or rough notes in directly below it. Copilot will read everything and produce a clean draft you can edit and send.

---

**Example 2 — Produce a plain-English summary of a legal document**

> "Summarise the key obligations and restrictions in this agreement in plain English. Focus on what our client must do, what they must not do, and any important deadlines. Avoid legal jargon — this summary is for a client who is not a lawyer."

Open the contract or agreement in Word first, then look for the Copilot button in the ribbon at the top of the screen and type this prompt into the Copilot panel that appears. Useful for client-facing summaries, onboarding packs, or briefings for non-legal colleagues.

---

**Example 3 — Identify potential issues in a draft**

> "Review this draft contract. Are there any clauses that appear one-sided, ambiguous, or that might cause problems for our client? List them with a brief explanation of the concern for each one."

Use this as a first-pass review on a draft before it goes to a senior reviewer. It will not replace a proper legal review, but it can flag obvious issues quickly and help you prepare your own questions.

## Copilot in Excel

Copilot in Excel can help you make sense of data without needing to be an Excel expert — useful for matter trackers, billing summaries, and any spreadsheet someone has handed you to review.

---

**Example 1 — Get a plain-English summary of a spreadsheet**

> "Summarise what this spreadsheet shows. What are the main totals, any standout figures, and is there anything here that looks unusual or worth flagging?"

Use this when someone sends you a spreadsheet and you need to understand it quickly before a meeting or sign-off.

---

**Example 2 — Find overdue or upcoming deadlines**

> "Look at the [Deadline] column. List any dates that have already passed and any that fall within the next 14 days. Include the [matter name] and [fee earner] from each row."

Before you type this, glance at the column headings in your spreadsheet and swap in the actual names — for example, your deadline column might be called "Due Date" and your people column might be called "Assigned To". Just replace the words in brackets with whatever your spreadsheet actually says. Useful for matter trackers or compliance logs where you need to spot what needs attention without scrolling through every row.

---

**Example 3 — Explain what a formula does**

> "Explain what the formula in the cell I have selected does, in plain English. I am not an Excel expert — keep the explanation simple."

How to use this: click on any cell that contains a formula (you will see an equals sign and some text appear in the bar just above the spreadsheet when you click it). Then open the Copilot panel and type the prompt above. No more guessing what a formula is doing or why a figure looks wrong — Copilot will explain it clearly, and you can ask follow-up questions.

## Quick Tips

**Open the document first, then ask.** For Word and Excel tasks, open the file and then look for the Copilot button in the ribbon at the top of the screen — click it to open the Copilot panel, then type your prompt there. This gives Copilot direct access to what is on your screen and produces much more accurate results than asking it to find the file on its own.

**Be specific about length.** "Write a short summary" is vague. "Write a three-sentence summary" is clear and gets you what you need.

**Tell it the audience.** "This is for the client" versus "this is for an internal team update" will produce noticeably different results. It is worth including.

**If the first result is not right, refine it.** Try: *"Focus only on the termination clauses"*, *"Make it shorter"*, or *"Use a more formal tone."* You do not need to start over — just keep the conversation going.

**Use it to start, not to finish.** Copilot gives you a strong first draft. A quick read-through before anything goes to a client or a partner is all it takes.

## A Note on Data Safety

Good news first: Microsoft 365 Copilot is safe to use for your everyday legal work. It operates entirely within FinBridge's own Microsoft environment — your documents and emails stay inside our systems, the same way they do when you use Outlook or Word normally.

The one habit worth keeping — and this carries over from the AI guidance in your Day 1 training — is this:

**Use Copilot (inside Microsoft 365) for work tasks. Do not use external AI tools such as ChatGPT or Google Gemini for anything work-related.**

The difference matters because external tools sit outside FinBridge's systems. If you paste a client's contract, matter details, or any confidential correspondence into an external tool, that information leaves our control. Copilot inside Microsoft 365 is the right tool for that work — it is specifically built and approved for it.

A simple way to remember it: if the information would feel sensitive to send in an email to someone outside the firm, use Copilot rather than an external tool.

If you spot a coloured confidentiality banner at the top of a document — for example "Confidential" or "Highly Confidential" — that is just a label that tells our systems how to handle the file. You do not need to do anything different; just treat the content with the same care you would any sensitive matter document.
